# Spanish legal package — for Brian's approval

**10 templates.** 9 are legal instruments
(Master + 6 Schedules + 2 §7216 consents); 1 is the covering email.
You said nine — nine is the legal count, and the email makes ten.

Every one is written to the database and **none is approved**. Spanish-speaking clients
still receive English until you approve each one individually in
**Admin → Templates → Approve ES**. That button says "this confirms you have READ the
translation and it says what the English says", which is the standard I have held these to.

---

## Read this before approving the two §7216 consents

Those two contain language the **IRS prescribes** (Treas. Reg. §301.7216 / Rev. Proc. 2013-14):
"Federal law requires this consent form be provided to you…", the invalid-if-conditioned
sentence, the duration rule, the TIGTA contact block.

**Translating mandated wording is a legal decision, not a linguistic one.** I translated it
faithfully, but I am not in a position to tell you the IRS will treat a Spanish rendering as
satisfying the mandatory-statement requirement. Options, in the order I would consider them:

1. Present the consent **bilingually** — Spanish for comprehension, English retained as the
   operative text. This is the common practice and the one I would pick.
2. Have your attorney approve the Spanish as standalone.
3. Keep the consents English-only and approve the other eight.

The other eight are ordinary contract translation and carry no equivalent constraint.

## One thing I did NOT do

None of these documents contains a governing-language clause ("the English version
controls"). The English originals do not have one either, so adding it to the Spanish would
be **inventing a legal term you never agreed to**. If you want it, that is a sentence for
your attorney to write and for both language versions to carry.
